# Method Notes

This scaffold demonstrates how authority effectiveness, governance risk, shadow-governance risk, and legitimacy-decay risk can be modeled while preserving interpretive caution.

## Conceptual model

The model treats authority effectiveness as an interaction among:

- formal legitimacy
- procedural fairness
- informational access
- practical influence
- ethical credibility
- accountability quality
- voice access
- oversight strength
- arbitrariness risk
- governance fragmentation
- distrust pressure
- informal power concentration

## Suggested methods

- descriptive diagnostics
- unit-level governance dashboards
- logistic regression for governance risk
- shadow-governance risk review
- legitimacy-decay risk review
- scenario analysis
- sensitivity analysis
- qualitative validation
- decision-rights audit
- information-flow mapping
- accountability review
- employee voice and psychological safety review
- conflict-of-interest and oversight review

## Methodological caution

Authority and power are partly measurable but not fully captured by metrics. They involve legitimacy, meaning, procedural justice, symbolic authority, trust, expertise, voice, accountability, fear, power asymmetry, hidden influence, history, and lived experience. Quantitative outputs should be paired with qualitative research and ethical governance.
