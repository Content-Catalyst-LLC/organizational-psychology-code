# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying cognitive bias as an institutional decision-system condition.

## Research question

How can cognitive bias pressure be represented, simulated, and reviewed as a system-level construct shaped by signal quality, viewpoint diversity, analytical discipline, structured challenge, hierarchy suppression, time pressure, routine reinforcement, learning review, and group conformity?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Cognitive bias is not only an individual-level mental error; it can become institutionalized through routines, power, hierarchy, metrics, culture, and decision processes.
2. Bias pressure increases when hierarchy suppresses dissent, time pressure compresses judgment, routines reinforce prior assumptions, and groups converge too quickly.
3. Bias pressure decreases when signal quality, viewpoint diversity, analytical discipline, structured challenge, and learning review are strong.
4. Decision errors and escalation of commitment should be treated as decision-system outcomes, not as simple personal failings.
5. Bias analytics must support institutional learning rather than employee surveillance, executive ranking, or retrospective blame.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a bias pressure score
- estimate decision error outcomes
- identify escalation-of-commitment risk
- summarize unit-level bias-risk conditions
- model scenario differences under high and low structured challenge
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through decision audits, governance review, meeting analysis, decision logs, postmortems, and interviews.
