# Ethics and Scope Note

This scaffold is for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, institutional learning, conflict-system review, mediation-process education, procedural fairness analysis, leadership and dispute-system analysis, and reproducible workflows.

It is not designed, validated, or appropriate for:

- employee screening
- employment selection
- hiring
- promotion
- compensation
- discipline
- termination
- workplace surveillance
- individual performance management
- cultural-loyalty scoring
- conflict-proneness scoring
- collaboration scoring of workers
- worker-risk scoring
- productivity ranking
- psychological assessment
- automated personnel decision-making

## Appropriate unit of analysis

The appropriate unit of analysis is the team environment, conflict system, dispute process, mediation pathway, organizational unit, governance structure, role system, institutional field, stakeholder relationship, or team-level conflict condition.

The workflow should never be used to label an individual worker as collaborative, uncollaborative, difficult, conflict-prone, safe, unsafe, loyal, disloyal, resistant, nonresistant, high-risk, suitable, unsuitable, or employable.

## Responsible interpretation

Conflict-system risk signals may reflect structural contradictions, unclear authority, weak procedural fairness, high blame intensity, low psychological safety, power asymmetry, retaliation memory, resource strain, poor role design, or weak mediation access.

## Anti-surveillance principle

Conflict analytics should increase institutional responsibility, not individual exposure. If a workflow causes people to fear naming conflict, unfairness, power asymmetry, bad faith, retaliation, or lived team experience, it is undermining the learning conditions needed for responsible conflict-system review.

## Governance requirement

Any real-world adaptation should include:

- informed governance
- worker and stakeholder consultation
- privacy review
- anti-retaliation safeguards
- transparency about purpose
- limits on use
- human interpretation
- rights-preserving oversight
