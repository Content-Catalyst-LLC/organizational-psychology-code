# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying conflict resolution as a team-level and institutional capability rather than an individual personality trait, conflict-proneness score, loyalty measure, or cultural-fit marker.

## Research question

How can constructive conflict capacity, escalation risk, mediation access, and collaborative resolution be represented, simulated, and reviewed as system-level constructs shaped by trust, communication clarity, psychological safety, procedural fairness, integrative skill, blame intensity, power asymmetry, and resource strain?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Conflict is a normal feature of organized life under interdependence and difference.
2. Conflict becomes constructive or destructive depending on trust, fairness, safety, power, communication, and system design.
3. Visible conflict frequency alone is not an indicator of organizational health; silence may indicate safety or fear.
4. Conflict resolution must distinguish task, process, relationship, structural, value, and power-related conflicts.
5. Conflict analytics must support institutional learning and dispute-system design rather than worker scoring, surveillance, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a constructive conflict capacity score
- estimate collaborative resolution outcomes
- identify symbolic resolution risk
- summarize team-level conflict-system conditions
- model escalation risk under blame, power asymmetry, and resource strain
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for team, mediation, and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through interviews, mediation-record review, grievance-pattern review, meeting observation, procedural fairness review, stakeholder consultation, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
