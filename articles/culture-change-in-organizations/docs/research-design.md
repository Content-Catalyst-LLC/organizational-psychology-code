# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying culture change as an institutional process rather than as values messaging or individual attitude compliance.

## Research question

How can culture change capacity be represented, simulated, and reviewed as a system-level construct shaped by leadership credibility, narrative coherence, reinforcement alignment, adaptive learning, psychological safety, identity adaptability, resistance intensity, subcultural fragmentation, legacy lock-in, and leadership turnover?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Culture change requires credible alignment between stated values and lived institutional behavior.
2. Culture is reproduced through reinforcement, rituals, stories, leadership conduct, decision rights, incentives, and organizational memory.
3. Successful cultural adoption depends on both meaning and material conditions.
4. Symbolic culture change occurs when language changes faster than reinforcement, governance, safety, or behavior.
5. Culture-change analytics must support institutional learning rather than individual cultural loyalty scoring or workplace surveillance.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a culture change capacity score
- estimate successful cultural adoption outcomes
- identify symbolic culture-change risk
- summarize unit-level culture-change conditions
- model culture risk
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through culture audits, interviews, observation, governance review, promotion-pattern review, exit interviews, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
