# Method Notes

This scaffold demonstrates how motivation quality, adaptive performance, burnout risk, and controlled-motivation risk can be modeled while preserving interpretive caution.

## Conceptual model

The model treats motivational quality as an interaction among:

- autonomy support
- competence development
- recognition quality
- institutional trust
- goal clarity
- fairness perception
- relatedness
- meaningful work
- psychological safety
- leadership credibility
- workload strain
- control pressure

## Suggested methods

- descriptive diagnostics
- unit-level motivational-system dashboards
- logistic regression for performance risk
- burnout-risk review
- controlled-motivation risk review
- scenario analysis
- sensitivity analysis
- qualitative validation
- work-design audit
- workload and staffing review
- leadership and trust review
- psychological safety assessment
- motivation climate review

## Methodological caution

Motivation is partly measurable but not fully captured by metrics. It involves meaning, autonomy, competence, belonging, recognition, fairness, trust, workload, identity, voice, leadership credibility, power, and lived experience. Quantitative outputs should be paired with qualitative research and ethical governance.
