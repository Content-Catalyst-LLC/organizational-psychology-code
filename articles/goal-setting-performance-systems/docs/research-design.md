# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying goal setting and performance systems as institutional, psychological, and governance systems rather than individual productivity scores, worker rankings, or automated performance judgments.

## Research question

How can goal system effectiveness, performance-system risk, goal clarity, challenge, feedback quality, employee commitment, strategic alignment, overload pressure, metric distortion, incentive misalignment, review quality, proxy drift, and goal feasibility be represented, simulated, and reviewed as unit-level and institutional constructs?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Goals direct attention, effort, persistence, and strategy formation.
2. Goal systems translate strategy into operational expectations.
3. Goals motivate only when they are clear, credible, fair, resourced, and connected to meaningful feedback.
4. Metrics are proxies, not reality itself.
5. Goal systems can create unintended consequences when they encourage metric fixation, overload, gaming, proxy drift, local optimization, or punitive monitoring.
6. Goal-system analytics must support institutional learning and metric governance rather than worker scoring, surveillance, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate goal system effectiveness scores
- estimate performance risk under goal-system conditions
- identify metric-gaming risk and symbolic alignment risk
- summarize unit-level goal-system review priorities
- model proxy drift, overload pressure, and incentive misalignment
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for unit, leadership, governance, and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through goal audits, strategy-map review, workload analysis, feedback-quality review, dashboard audit, incentive review, cross-functional retrospectives, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
