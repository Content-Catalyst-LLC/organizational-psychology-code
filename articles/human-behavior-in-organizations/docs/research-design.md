# Research Design

This scaffold supports methods demonstration for studying human behavior in organizations as a person-system interaction.

## Research question

How can behavior quality, voice behavior, and behavior-system risk be represented as products of capability, motivation, role clarity, psychological safety, leadership trust, cultural norms, incentive alignment, workload pressure, burnout pressure, hierarchical pressure, perceived voice efficacy, and fear of retaliation?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Behavior in organizations is shaped by individual, team, and institutional conditions.
2. Low motivation, silence, conflict, or performance strain may reflect system conditions rather than individual deficiency.
3. Voice depends on psychological safety, leadership trust, perceived efficacy, hierarchy, and fear.
4. Behavior-system risk is a system-level construct, not a worker-risk label.
5. Organizational psychology analytics must support institutional learning and responsible work-system improvement.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- create synthetic organizational behavior data
- summarize behavior-system conditions by unit
- model behavior quality from individual and system variables
- model voice behavior without identifying dissenters
- compare high-trust and high-risk organizational scenarios
- document ethical boundaries and responsible-use constraints

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic unit profile should not be read as a real-world finding without qualitative validation, participatory review, privacy safeguards, anti-retaliation protections, and attention to workload, role design, leadership behavior, culture, and power.
