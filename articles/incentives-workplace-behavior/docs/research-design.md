# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying incentive systems as institutional, psychological, cultural, and ethical systems rather than individual productivity scores, worker rankings, incentive-compliance measures, or automated personnel judgments.

## Research question

How can incentive effectiveness, performance-system risk, fairness perception, expectancy strength, strategic alignment, intrinsic support, feedback clarity, distortion risk, overload pressure, ethical risk, cooperation support, recognition legitimacy, reward transparency, and hidden-labor recognition be represented, simulated, and reviewed as unit-level and institutional constructs?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Incentives do not merely reward behavior; they teach employees what the institution values.
2. Employees interpret rewards through fairness, expectancy, trust, identity, culture, and social comparison.
3. Incentives can support motivation when they align with meaningful work, fair evaluation, clear feedback, and credible institutional purpose.
4. Incentives can distort behavior when rewards are narrow, unfair, opaque, overly competitive, ethically risky, or detached from real value.
5. Metrics are proxies, not reality itself.
6. Incentive-system analytics must support institutional learning and reward governance rather than worker scoring, surveillance, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate incentive effectiveness scores
- estimate performance risk under incentive-system conditions
- identify incentive gaming risk and symbolic reward risk
- summarize unit-level reward-governance review priorities
- model fairness, alignment, overload, ethical risk, and distortion
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for unit, leadership, governance, and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through incentive-plan audits, compensation review, promotion and recognition review, workload analysis, feedback-quality review, ethics review, cross-functional retrospectives, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
