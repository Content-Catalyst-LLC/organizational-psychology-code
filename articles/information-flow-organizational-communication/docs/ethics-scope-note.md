# Ethics and Scope Note

This scaffold is for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, institutional learning, organizational communication review, knowledge-flow governance, decision-process review, and reproducible workflows.

It is not designed, validated, or appropriate for:

- employee screening
- employment selection
- hiring
- promotion
- compensation
- discipline
- termination
- workplace surveillance
- individual performance management
- communication surveillance
- message monitoring
- productivity ranking
- psychological assessment
- automated personnel decision-making

## Appropriate unit of analysis

The appropriate unit of analysis is the organization, communication system, work system, knowledge-flow architecture, decision process, governance structure, or institutional condition.

The workflow should never be used to label an individual worker as responsive, unresponsive, communicative, noncommunicative, productive, unproductive, high-risk, suitable, unsuitable, psychologically safe, psychologically unsafe, loyal, disloyal, or employable.

## Responsible interpretation

Communication risk signals may reflect structural underinvestment, unclear escalation pathways, fear of retaliation, poor tool governance, overload, hierarchy delay, weak documentation, status filtering, distorted incentives, or inadequate cross-functional translation.

## Anti-surveillance principle

Communication analytics should increase institutional responsibility, not individual exposure. If a workflow causes people to fear raising concerns, asking questions, reporting errors, or communicating honestly, it is undermining the learning conditions needed for responsible organizational communication.

## Governance requirement

Any real-world adaptation should include:

- informed governance
- worker consultation
- privacy review
- anti-retaliation safeguards
- transparency about purpose
- limits on use
- human interpretation
- rights-preserving oversight
