# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying information flow and organizational communication as an institutional and system-level process.

## Research question

How can information flow quality be represented, simulated, and reviewed as a system-level construct shaped by signal quality, timeliness, interpretive clarity, cross-functional connectivity, psychological safety, distortion risk, overload pressure, hierarchy delay, and external signal turbulence?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Information flow is not reducible to message volume, platform activity, or individual responsiveness.
2. Communication quality depends on whether relevant knowledge becomes visible, credible, interpretable, timely, and actionable.
3. Information can exist somewhere in an organization without becoming institutional knowledge.
4. Silence, hierarchy delay, overload, distortion, and weak psychological safety can make institutions blind to their own reality.
5. Communication analytics must support institutional learning rather than worker surveillance.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate an information flow quality score
- estimate decision error outcomes
- identify escalation failure risk
- summarize unit-level communication conditions
- model communication risk
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through communication audits, incident reviews, interviews, workflow mapping, governance review, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
