# Job Satisfaction and Organizational Commitment

This companion scaffold supports the article **“Job Satisfaction and Organizational Commitment.”** It provides synthetic datasets, reproducible workflows, documentation, validation notes, and multi-language examples for studying job satisfaction and organizational commitment as unit-level and institutional conditions shaped by leadership support, reward fairness, developmental opportunity, workload balance, institutional trust, cultural alignment, role insecurity, exhaustion pressure, affective commitment, continuance commitment, normative commitment, and retention risk.

The scaffold treats satisfaction and commitment not as employee positivity, loyalty, obedience, individual morale scoring, flight-risk scoring, or psychological assessment. It treats them as work-attitude and institutional signals that can help organizations examine whether work is meaningful, fair, developmental, sustainable, trustworthy, and worthy of durable attachment.

## Research purpose

This repository is designed for:

- organizational psychology methods demonstration
- synthetic-data modeling
- job satisfaction and commitment diagnostics
- work-attitude and retention-system analysis
- leadership support and fairness review
- workload sustainability analysis
- institutional trust and cultural alignment review
- affective, continuance, and normative commitment education
- responsible and reproducible organizational analytics

It is not designed for employee screening, cultural-loyalty scoring, commitment scoring of workers, retention-risk scoring of individual employees, psychological assessment, productivity ranking, workplace surveillance, employment decision-making, or individual risk labeling.

## Conceptual frame

The synthetic workflow models job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and retention risk as system-level constructs shaped by:

- leadership support
- reward fairness
- developmental opportunity
- workload balance
- institutional trust
- cultural alignment
- role insecurity
- exhaustion pressure
- affective commitment
- continuance commitment
- normative commitment
- healthy retention conditions
- constrained retention risk
- symbolic commitment risk

## Responsible-use notice

These materials are for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, institutional learning, work-attitude review, retention-system education, fairness and workload analysis, leadership and culture review, and reproducible workflows.

They are **not** employee-screening tools, employment-selection tools, hiring tools, promotion tools, compensation tools, discipline tools, termination tools, workplace surveillance systems, individual performance-management systems, cultural-loyalty scoring tools, commitment scoring tools, retention-risk scoring tools for individual workers, productivity-ranking tools, psychological assessment tools, or automated personnel decision-making systems.

The appropriate unit of analysis is the work system, organizational unit, leadership system, fairness system, workload system, role design, institutional trust condition, culture, retention environment, or organization-level attachment condition—not the worth, loyalty, commitment, employability, morality, psychological status, productivity, or legitimacy of any individual employee.
