# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying leadership as an institutional, relational, ethical, multilevel, and systems-level condition rather than a charisma score, leader popularity score, employee attitude score, loyalty score, political-influence score, or automated personnel judgment.

## Research question

How can leadership capacity, institutional stability, trust generation, communication clarity, motivational support, legitimacy quality, adaptive coordination, psychological safety, role clarity, ethical accountability, decision transparency, voice access, ambiguity pressure, distrust pressure, fragmentation pressure, overload pressure, voice-suppression risk, legitimacy decay, and performance risk be represented, simulated, and reviewed as unit-level and institutional constructs?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Leadership is a social influence process embedded in organizational systems.
2. Leadership operates across individual, dyadic, team, institutional, and system-of-systems levels.
3. Leadership capacity depends on trust, communication, motivation, legitimacy, psychological safety, role clarity, ethical accountability, and adaptive coordination.
4. Institutional stability is weakened by ambiguity, distrust, fragmentation, overload, voice suppression, and legitimacy decay.
5. Leadership analytics must support institutional learning and leadership-system improvement rather than worker scoring, dissent tracking, or employment decisions.
