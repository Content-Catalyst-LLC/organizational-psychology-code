# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying adaptive organizational change as a system-level institutional capability.

## Research question

How can adaptive change capacity be represented, simulated, and reviewed as a system-level construct shaped by sensing quality, interpretive coherence, governance alignment, learning capability, resource flexibility, institutional legitimacy, inertia, fragmentation, resistance friction, and environmental pressure?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Organizational change is not merely implementation; it is interpretation, coordination, legitimacy, governance, and learning.
2. Adaptive organizations preserve useful continuity while revising structures and assumptions that no longer fit reality.
3. Successful transformation depends on institutional capacity, not only leadership vision.
4. Symbolic change can occur when formal activity is high but routines, incentives, legitimacy, or governance do not change.
5. Change analytics must support institutional learning rather than worker surveillance.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate an adaptive change capacity score
- estimate successful transformation outcomes
- identify symbolic change risk
- summarize unit-level adaptation conditions
- model adaptation risk
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through interviews, implementation review, governance analysis, workload review, and participatory sensemaking with affected stakeholders.
