# Organizational Culture and Shared Norms

This companion scaffold supports the article **“Organizational Culture and Shared Norms.”** It provides synthetic datasets, reproducible workflows, documentation, validation notes, and multi-language examples for studying organizational culture as a system-level pattern of shared meaning, norms, leadership signals, psychological safety, value-practice alignment, subcultural variation, contradiction, incentive distortion, learning capacity, and legitimacy risk.

The scaffold treats organizational culture not as branding, employee sentiment, morale, or individual “culture fit,” but as an institutional condition shaped by value alignment, normative consistency, leadership credibility, psychological safety, shared meaning, contradiction, fragmentation, incentive distortion, external pressure, and institutional performance integrity.

## Research purpose

This repository is designed for:

- organizational psychology methods demonstration
- synthetic-data modeling
- culture diagnostics
- shared-norms analysis
- subcultural variation review
- psychological safety and learning analysis
- leadership credibility and values-alignment analysis
- culture-risk and symbolic-culture review
- institutional learning and accountability review
- responsible and reproducible organizational analytics

It is not designed for employee scoring, cultural-loyalty scoring, cultural-fit screening, workplace surveillance, psychological assessment, productivity ranking, employment decision-making, or individual alignment labeling.

## Conceptual frame

The synthetic workflow models cultural coherence and institutional performance integrity as system-level constructs shaped by:

- value alignment
- normative consistency
- leadership credibility
- psychological safety
- shared meaning
- contradiction
- fragmentation
- incentive distortion
- external pressure
- cultural learning capacity
- performance integrity
- symbolic culture risk

## Responsible-use notice

These materials are for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, institutional learning, culture review, organizational development education, legitimacy analysis, and reproducible workflows.

They are **not** employee-screening tools, employment-selection tools, hiring tools, promotion tools, compensation tools, discipline tools, termination tools, workplace surveillance systems, individual performance-management systems, cultural-loyalty scoring tools, cultural-fit tools, productivity-ranking tools, psychological assessment tools, or automated personnel decision-making systems.

The appropriate unit of analysis is the organization, cultural system, governance structure, institutional field, stakeholder relationship, organizational unit, subculture, work system, or legitimacy condition—not the worth, loyalty, identity, attitude, cultural fit, employability, psychological status, or moral status of any individual worker.
