# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying organizational identity and institutional legitimacy as system-level constructs rather than branding, image management, or individual loyalty.

## Research question

How can organizational identity coherence and institutional legitimacy be represented, simulated, and reviewed as system-level constructs shaped by mission clarity, value-practice alignment, narrative continuity, leadership credibility, behavioral consistency, internal fragmentation, identity contradiction, and external legitimacy pressure?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Organizational identity is a shared institutional self-understanding, not merely a public brand.
2. Institutional legitimacy depends partly on the perceived coherence between identity claims and observed conduct.
3. Identity coherence weakens when mission, values, leadership behavior, and routine practice diverge.
4. Strong identity can support resilience, but it can also produce rigidity if it resists needed adaptation.
5. Identity analytics must support institutional learning and accountability rather than worker loyalty scoring or surveillance.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate an identity coherence score
- estimate stakeholder legitimacy outcomes
- identify symbolic identity risk
- summarize unit-level identity-legitimacy conditions
- model stakeholder trust under contradiction and fragmentation
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through identity review, stakeholder interviews, governance review, document analysis, historical review, culture review, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
