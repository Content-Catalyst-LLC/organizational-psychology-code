# Ethics and Scope Note

This scaffold is for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, institutional learning, organizational psychology education, complex behavioral systems analysis, work-system review, feedback-loop modeling, learning/resilience/fragmentation/silence-risk review, and reproducible workflows.

It is not designed, validated, or appropriate for:

- employee screening
- employment selection
- hiring
- promotion
- compensation
- discipline
- termination
- workplace surveillance
- individual performance management
- productivity ranking
- loyalty scoring
- dissent tracking
- psychological assessment
- automated personnel decision-making

## Appropriate unit of analysis

The appropriate unit of analysis is the work system, team environment, organizational unit, leadership system, communication routine, feedback loop, role-design condition, psychological-safety climate, learning system, resilience condition, accountability structure, or institutional context.

The workflow should never be used to label an individual worker as productive, unproductive, loyal, disloyal, motivated, unmotivated, dissenting, compliant, psychologically deficient, high-risk, low-risk, suitable, unsuitable, or employable.

## Responsible interpretation

Complex system signals may reflect unclear roles, weak leadership trust, poor communication, excessive workload, inadequate support, low psychological safety, cultural contradiction, fear of retaliation, silence risk, fragmentation, weak learning capacity, low redundancy, or institutional constraints.

## Anti-surveillance principle

Complex behavioral systems analysis should increase institutional responsibility, not individual exposure. If a workflow causes people to fear naming workload, distrust, role ambiguity, weak leadership, burnout, unfairness, unsafe voice conditions, fragmentation, or fear of retaliation, it is undermining responsible organizational learning.
