# Research Design

This scaffold supports methods demonstration for studying organizations as complex behavioral systems.

## Research question

How can behavior quality, complex system risk, learning capacity, resilience, feedback, fragmentation, silence risk, workload pressure, information flow, and environmental uncertainty be represented as system-level constructs in organizational psychology?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Organizational behavior emerges from person-system interaction.
2. Complex system risk is produced by interacting pressures, not a single isolated variable.
3. Trust, psychological safety, information flow, learning capacity, and redundancy support resilience.
4. Overload, silence, fragmentation, low trust, weak learning, and uncertainty can reinforce fragility.
5. System-risk models must support institutional learning and responsible work-system improvement, not individual surveillance.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- create synthetic complex behavioral system data
- summarize risk and resilience conditions by unit
- model behavior quality from individual and system variables
- compare healthy learning systems with fragmented overloaded systems
- document ethical boundaries and responsible-use constraints

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic unit profile should not be read as a real-world finding without qualitative validation, participatory review, privacy safeguards, anti-retaliation protections, and attention to workload, role design, leadership behavior, culture, feedback loops, networks, and power.
