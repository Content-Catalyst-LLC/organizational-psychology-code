# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying psychological safety as a team-level and institutional condition rather than an individual personality trait, comfort score, loyalty measure, or cultural-fit marker.

## Research question

How can psychological safety, voice behavior, error-reporting climate, and team learning capacity be represented, simulated, and reviewed as system-level constructs shaped by leadership openness, inclusion quality, learning review quality, knowledge sharing, blame intensity, status asymmetry, perceived penalty, and workload pressure?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Psychological safety is a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking.
2. Psychological safety is compatible with high standards, accountability, rigor, and difficult feedback.
3. Voice behavior is shaped by leadership response, status dynamics, prior retaliation memory, and perceived usefulness of speaking.
4. Error reporting may increase when safety improves; low reporting can reflect fear rather than low error.
5. Psychological safety analytics must support team learning and institutional design rather than worker scoring, surveillance, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a psychological safety score
- estimate strong team performance outcomes
- identify symbolic voice risk
- summarize team-level safety conditions
- model performance integrity under blame and status asymmetry
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for team and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through interviews, meeting observation, incident-reporting review, psychological safety survey review, retrospective analysis, leadership behavior review, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
