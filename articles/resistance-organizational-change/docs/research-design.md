# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying resistance to organizational change as an institutional and system-level phenomenon.

## Research question

How can resistance to organizational change be represented, simulated, and reviewed as a system-level construct shaped by perceived loss, uncertainty, identity threat, routine embeddedness, power displacement, trust, participation, implementation clarity, workload strain, and prior failed change history?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Resistance is not reducible to individual attitude.
2. Resistance may contain information about trust, feasibility, legitimacy, workload, culture, power, and institutional memory.
3. Change adoption depends on the social architecture of implementation, not only the technical merit of the proposed reform.
4. Resistance can be constructive, defensive, political, symbolic, protective, or obstructive depending on context.
5. Resistance analytics must be ethically bounded so they support institutional learning rather than employee surveillance.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a resistance intensity score
- estimate constructive adoption
- identify symbolic compliance risk
- produce unit-level change-readiness summaries
- model change-friction risk
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through interviews, implementation review, workload analysis, governance review, and participatory sensemaking with affected stakeholders.
