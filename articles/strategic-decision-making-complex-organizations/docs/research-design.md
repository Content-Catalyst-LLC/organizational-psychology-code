# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying strategic decision-making as an institutional and system-level process.

## Research question

How can strategic decision quality be represented, simulated, and reviewed as a system-level construct shaped by environmental sensing, interpretive coherence, knowledge integration, option diversity, governance quality, adaptive learning, bias pressure, political distortion, complexity load, and environmental turbulence?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Strategic decision-making is not reducible to individual executive talent.
2. Strategy is shaped by institutional sensing, framing, knowledge integration, governance, power, and learning.
3. Strategic decision quality can be strengthened by broader option generation, stronger challenge norms, better information flow, and adaptive review.
4. Symbolic strategy can occur when formal plans, language, or commitments exist without meaningful learning, governance, or implementation discipline.
5. Strategic analytics must support institutional learning rather than executive ranking, personnel control, or retrospective blame.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a strategic decision quality score
- estimate successful strategy outcomes
- identify symbolic strategy risk
- summarize unit-level strategic review conditions
- model strategic risk
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through strategic review, decision archives, board materials, interviews, postmortems, stakeholder analysis, and governance review.
