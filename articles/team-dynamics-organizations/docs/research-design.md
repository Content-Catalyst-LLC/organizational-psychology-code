# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying team dynamics as relational, structural, and institutional conditions rather than individual personality traits, team-fit scores, loyalty measures, worker collaboration scores, or generic teamwork sentiment.

## Research question

How can team effectiveness, team-system risk, communication quality, role clarity, psychological safety, leadership support, conflict load, task ambiguity, coordination cost, participation equity, and strong collective performance be represented, simulated, and reviewed as system-level constructs?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Team dynamics are patterned interactions through which groups transform individual expertise into collective performance.
2. Teams do not simply aggregate talent; they require communication, trust, role clarity, coordination, learning routines, and psychological safety.
3. Weak team dynamics may reflect organizational design, workload, unclear authority, poor communication architecture, or leadership conditions rather than individual attitude.
4. A quiet or harmonious team may be aligned, but it may also be avoidant or unsafe.
5. Team analytics must support institutional learning and team-system design rather than worker scoring, surveillance, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a team effectiveness score
- estimate strong collective performance outcomes
- identify symbolic teamwork risk
- summarize team-level dynamics and review priorities
- model team-system risk under conflict load, ambiguity, and coordination cost
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for team, leadership, and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through interviews, meeting observation, retrospective review, communication audits, role mapping, decision-log review, workload analysis, psychological safety review, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
