# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying transformational leadership as an institutional, behavioral, relational, ethical, and change-system condition rather than a charisma score, positivity score, loyalty score, or automated personnel judgment.

## Research question

How can transformational capacity, adaptive performance, vision credibility, motivational alignment, intellectual stimulation, developmental support, institutional trust, psychological safety, change communication, implementation support, meaning-making quality, resistance intensity, ambiguity pressure, change fatigue, voice-suppression risk, and purpose-exploitation risk be represented, simulated, and reviewed as unit-level and institutional constructs?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Transformational leadership mobilizes change through vision, meaning, trust, learning, and development.
2. Transformation requires psychological alignment, not only strategic announcement or structural redesign.
3. Vision must be credible, resourced, participatory, and ethically governed.
4. Intellectual stimulation requires psychological safety and usable implementation pathways.
5. Change fatigue, ambiguity, and low trust can weaken transformational capacity even when the vision is compelling.
6. Transformational-leadership analytics must support institutional learning and change-system improvement rather than worker scoring, dissent tracking, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate transformational capacity scores
- estimate adaptive performance risk under change-system conditions
- identify voice-suppression risk, style-misfit risk, and purpose-exploitation risk
- summarize unit-level transformational leadership review priorities
- model vision, trust, stimulation, support, ambiguity, fatigue, and resistance
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for leadership development, change governance, unit review, organizational learning, psychological safety review, communication review, and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through employee voice review, leadership interviews, change-readiness assessment, meeting observation, decision-process audit, workload review, psychological-safety review, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
