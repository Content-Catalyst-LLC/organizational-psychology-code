# Research Design

This scaffold supports research-methods demonstration for studying trust and cooperation as relational and institutional infrastructures rather than individual personality traits, loyalty measures, worker trustworthiness scores, or generic teamwork sentiment.

## Research question

How can cooperative capacity, trust risk, reciprocity, communication reliability, and strong collaboration be represented, simulated, and reviewed as system-level constructs shaped by competence trust, integrity trust, benevolence trust, reciprocity norms, communication reliability, opportunism risk, power imbalance, uncertainty load, leadership credibility, and institutional trust?

## Conceptual assumptions

1. Trust is a willingness to accept vulnerability based on expectations of competence, integrity, benevolence, fairness, and non-exploitation.
2. Cooperation is coordinated action under interdependence, not friendliness or mere compliance.
3. Trust can be interpersonal, team-level, cross-functional, leadership-based, and institutional.
4. Distrust is not always irrational; it may reflect accurate experience of unfairness, betrayal, or opportunistic systems.
5. Cooperation can be inequitable if burdens and risks are distributed unevenly.
6. Trust analytics must support institutional learning and better collaboration-system design rather than worker scoring, surveillance, or employment decisions.

## Synthetic modeling aims

The scaffold demonstrates how to:

- generate a cooperative capacity score
- estimate strong collaboration outcomes
- identify symbolic cooperation risk
- summarize team-level trust and cooperation conditions
- model collaboration risk under opportunism, power imbalance, and uncertainty
- document responsible-use boundaries

## Appropriate interpretation

Outputs should be interpreted as prompts for team, leadership, and institutional inquiry. A high-risk synthetic profile should not be read as a real-world diagnosis. In actual organizational research, quantitative signals require qualitative validation through interviews, meeting observation, governance review, decision follow-through, cross-functional retrospectives, workload review, psychological safety review, and participatory interpretation with affected groups.
