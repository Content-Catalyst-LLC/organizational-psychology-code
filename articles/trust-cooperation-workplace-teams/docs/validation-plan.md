# Validation Plan

This validation plan applies to synthetic methods demonstration. It does not validate a real-world employment, personnel, surveillance, trustworthiness-scoring, cooperation-scoring, performance-ranking, or psychological assessment tool.

## 1. Construct validation

Review whether variables represent trust-system and institutional conditions rather than individual worker traits.

Key questions:

- Does the measure describe a relationship, team climate, collaboration system, or institutional condition?
- Does it describe competence trust, integrity trust, benevolence trust, reciprocity, communication reliability, opportunism risk, power imbalance, or institutional trust?
- Does it describe the environment rather than an individual's personality, loyalty, or trustworthiness?
- Could it be misused to label or rank individuals?

## 2. Data quality review

In real-world research, examine:

- source provenance
- fear of retaliation
- underreporting of distrust
- leadership self-report bias
- aggregate scores hiding marginalized experience
- uneven burdens of cooperation
- hidden support labor
- decision follow-through
- governance and accountability records
- context behind numeric indicators

## 3. Model validation

Assess:

- coefficient stability
- sensitivity to weighting choices
- uncertainty
- outlier influence
- subgroup interpretability
- qualitative plausibility
- whether model outputs match trust-and-cooperation review evidence

## 4. Ethical validation

Confirm that the workflow cannot reasonably be repurposed for individual worker monitoring, loyalty scoring, trustworthiness scoring, cooperation scoring, discipline, promotion decisions, compensation decisions, termination, or employment decisions.

## 5. Action validation

A trust-and-cooperation review is only useful if it leads to responsible institutional improvement. Track whether findings lead to:

- stronger communication reliability
- better reciprocity and burden-sharing
- reduced opportunism risk
- stronger leadership credibility
- stronger institutional trust
- clearer cross-functional expectations
- better trust repair mechanisms
- more accountable governance
